## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

### What BYD does

BYD is the world's largest manufacturer of new energy vehicles (NEVs) — battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) and plug-in hybrids (PHEVs) — and the second-largest maker of electric-vehicle batteries after CATL. It is also the world's largest integrator of battery energy storage systems (BESS) and, through its Hong Kong-listed subsidiary BYD Electronic (0285.HK), a major contract manufacturer of smartphone components and assembly. The company stopped building pure internal-combustion vehicles in 2022, so effectively every car it sells today is either a BEV or a PHEV.

What distinguishes BYD from almost every other automaker is the depth of its vertical integration. BYD designs and manufactures its own battery cells (the LFP "Blade" battery, now in its second generation), electric motors, power electronics, silicon-carbide power semiconductors (via BYD Semiconductor), vehicle platforms, driver-assistance software ("God's Eye") and, increasingly, its own charging network and ocean shipping fleet. That integration is the root of its cost advantage, its speed of product iteration and its ability to absorb price cuts that have pushed weaker Chinese rivals out of the market.

### How BYD makes money

Revenue comes from two reporting segments:

- **Automobiles and related products** (~80% of revenue): sales of passenger NEVs under five brands (BYD Dynasty and Ocean series, Denza, Fangchengbao and Yangwang), commercial vehicles (electric buses, trucks, SkyRail/monorail systems), batteries sold to third parties and energy storage systems. In H1 2026, auto and related revenue was RMB 275.3bn, down 9.0% year on year and close to 80% of group revenue [10].
- **Mobile handset components, assembly and other products** (~20%): BYD Electronic's smartphone, tablet, AI-server and new-energy-vehicle electronics businesses. This segment generated RMB 69.4bn in H1 2026, up 1.0% and 20.1% of revenue [9].

The vehicle business is a classic manufacturing model — BYD sells cars at wholesale to a network of franchised dealers (in China and most export markets) and to distributors or its own subsidiaries overseas. It earns incremental income from auto finance (BYD Auto Finance), insurance (BYD Property & Casualty Insurance), parts, and from 2026 a paid flash-charging network.

In 2025 BYD sold 4,602,436 NEVs, meeting a target that had been cut to 4.6 million from an original 5.5 million, and entered the top five global automotive groups by volume for the first time [5][6]. Revenue crossed RMB 800bn for the first time (RMB 803.97bn, +3.5%), but net profit fell 19% to RMB 32.62bn — its first annual decline since 2021 — as group gross margin compressed to 17.74% from 19.44% [5]. R&D spending reached a record RMB 63.4bn, roughly 7.9% of revenue and nearly twice net profit [6].

### Geographic footprint

BYD has transformed from a China-centric business into a genuinely global one in under three years. Overseas passenger car and pickup sales were 1.05 million units in 2025 (+145%), generating RMB 310.7bn of overseas revenue (+40%), or 38.7% of the group total [7]. In H1 2026 overseas revenue reached RMB 181.3bn — 52.6% of the group — exceeding domestic revenue for the first time in BYD's history [10]. Year to October 2026, exports were 1.51 million units (+94%), while domestic sales fell 28% to 2.11 million [14].

Manufacturing is still overwhelmingly Chinese (Shenzhen, Xi'an, Changsha, Hefei, Changzhou, Zhengzhou and others), with overseas passenger-car plants in Thailand (Rayong), Uzbekistan and Brazil (Camaçari) already operating, Hungary (Szeged) starting series production in Q4 2026, and Indonesia and Turkey at various stages of planning [6][25].

### The current investment debate in one paragraph

BYD is a company in transition. Its home market has turned hostile — the Chinese passenger-car market shrank about 20% in H1 2026 after the NEV purchase-tax exemption was halved — and its domestic per-vehicle economics have collapsed under a two-year price war. At the same time, exports have nearly doubled and carry materially higher margins: H1 2026 overseas gross margin was 21.7% versus 15.7% domestically [10]. The Q3 2026 result (net profit +28% to RMB 10.02bn) showed earnings can grow again on the back of export mix, but it still missed even the lowest broker forecast, inventory has risen 60% since December, and two policy headwinds — EU duties on Chinese PHEVs and the cut in China's NEV export VAT rebate from April 2027 — threaten exactly the profit pool that is driving the recovery [1][3][32].

## 2. COMPANY HISTORY

### Founding (1995–2002): a battery company

BYD was founded in February 1995 in Shenzhen by Wang Chuanfu, a 29-year-old chemist and government battery researcher, together with his cousin Lu Xiangyang, who provided a loan of about RMB 250,000 [16][17]. Wang had seen an opening as Japanese producers retreated from nickel-cadmium batteries; BYD's insight was to replace expensive automated production lines with labour-intensive, semi-automated lines that cut capital cost dramatically. Within a few years the company was supplying rechargeable batteries to Motorola, Nokia and Samsung, and by the end of the decade it had taken a large share of the global mobile-phone battery market from Japanese incumbents. BYD added lithium-ion production in 1998 and listed its H-shares in Hong Kong in 2002.

### Entering autos (2003–2008)

In 2003 BYD bought a controlling stake in Xi'an Qinchuan Automobile — a move that was poorly received by investors at the time. Wang hired Lian Yubo, then a young chief engineer, to build an automotive R&D organisation almost from scratch [24]. The first volume hit, the F3 compact sedan (2005), was widely dismissed as a Corolla imitation but sold strongly on price; follow-ons such as the F3R and F6 established BYD as a credible domestic brand [24]. In 2008 BYD launched the F3DM, among the world's first mass-production plug-in hybrids.

The same year, on the recommendation of Charlie Munger (introduced to Wang by Himalaya Capital's Li Lu), Berkshire Hathaway bought 225 million H-shares — roughly 10% of the company — for about US$230m [29][30]. The Berkshire endorsement gave BYD global visibility far beyond its size at the time.

### Building the platform (2009–2019)

The following decade combined ambition with setbacks. BYD launched electric buses and taxis (the K9 bus and e6 taxi), opened North American operations (Stella Li relocated the Americas headquarters to Los Angeles in 2011 and built bus and battery plants in Lancaster, California, in 2013) [22], formed the Denza premium JV with Daimler, and listed A-shares in Shenzhen in 2011. Profitability was volatile: when China cut EV subsidies between 2017 and 2019, BYD's sales and margins suffered, and net profit declined for three consecutive years [16].

Two lessons from that decade still shape BYD today. First, commercial vehicles — buses in particular — gave BYD early scale in batteries and electric drivetrains and a foothold in municipal procurement in Europe, Latin America and Asia long before its passenger cars were exported. Second, the subsidy cliff taught management that policy-driven demand can disappear overnight; the company's response was to drive down cost until its products could compete with ICE cars unsubsidised, which is the strategy that ultimately produced the Blade battery and DM-i. Wang had set a goal in 2008 for BYD to become China's leading automaker by 2015 and the world's largest by 2025 [17a] — a target that looked fanciful at the time and was largely met in NEV terms, if not in total vehicles.

### The NEV breakout (2020–2024)

Three decisions reset the company's trajectory:

1. **The Blade battery (2020):** a cell-to-pack LFP design that improved safety and space efficiency without nickel or cobalt, giving BYD a structurally cheaper battery than rivals using NMC chemistry.
2. **DM-i super-hybrid (2021):** a PHEV architecture with very low fuel consumption at ICE-equivalent prices, which opened the mass market to BYD in lower-tier Chinese cities.
3. **Exiting ICE (March 2022):** BYD became the first major automaker to stop producing pure combustion vehicles.

Volumes exploded: NEV sales went from 189,689 in 2020 to 603,783 in 2021 and 1.86 million in 2022 [5], then ~3.0 million in 2023 and ~4.27 million in 2024. Revenue grew 96%, 42% and 29% in 2022–2024 respectively, and net profit peaked at RMB 40.25bn in 2024 [7][11]. BYD launched the premium Yangwang and off-road Fangchengbao brands in 2023, overtook Tesla in quarterly BEV sales in Q4 2023 and began aggressive overseas expansion with a Thai plant (2024), dedicated car-carrier ships and entry into Europe, Latin America, Southeast Asia, Australia and Japan.

### Recent developments (2025–2026): price war, policy shock, export pivot

- **February 2025:** BYD made its "God's Eye" driver-assistance system standard on 21 models, including the Seagull from RMB 69,800, escalating the price war into ADAS [28].
- **2025:** Berkshire Hathaway completed its exit (confirmed via filings showing a zero holding as of 31 March 2025) [31]. BYD cut its 2025 sales target to 4.6 million, and full-year profit fell 19% [5]. The company reportedly reduced headcount by roughly 100,000 during the year [7].
- **March 2026:** launch of the second-generation Blade Battery and FLASH charging (10–70% in five minutes), with a target of 20,000 flash-charging stations in China by end-2026 [19][20].
- **Q1 2026:** net profit collapsed 55% to RMB 4.09bn as China's purchase-tax change and the price war hit simultaneously — the steepest quarterly decline since 2020 [12].
- **H1 2026:** overseas revenue exceeded domestic revenue for the first time [10].
- **Q3 2026:** net profit +28% to RMB 10.02bn, the first quarter of year-on-year revenue growth after four consecutive declines, but below consensus [1][2].
- **October 2026:** record month for 2026 (490,168 NEVs); BEV sales above 300,000 for the first time; exports +103% [14].
- **Q4 2026:** first European passenger-car plant (Szeged, Hungary) entering series production about a year behind schedule; Turkish plant on hold [25].

## 3. MANAGEMENT TEAM

BYD remains a founder-led company with an unusually long-tenured, internally grown senior team. Most of its top executives joined in the 1990s or early 2000s and have spent their entire careers there.

### Wang Chuanfu — Chairman, Executive Director and President (CEO)

Wang Chuanfu, 60, founded BYD in February 1995 and has run it as Chairman and President ever since — more than 31 years at the helm, making him one of the longest-serving founder-CEOs in the global auto industry. He was born in April 1966 into a poor farming family in Wuwei County, Anhui; both his parents died while he was young and he was raised by his elder siblings [16][17]. He studied metallurgical physical chemistry at Central South University of Technology (now Central South University), graduating in 1987, and earned a master's degree in materials in 1990 from the Beijing General Research Institute for Non-Ferrous Metals (now GRINM Group) [16][18].

Before founding BYD, Wang was vice supervisor at the Beijing Non-Ferrous Research Institute (1990–1995) and briefly general manager of Shenzhen Bige Battery (1993–1994), a battery company affiliated with the institute [18]. He founded BYD with a small family loan and built it into a top-tier global supplier of phone batteries within a decade, then made the contrarian decision to enter automobiles in 2003. His strategic record since then includes three industry-shaping calls: betting on LFP chemistry when the industry was moving to NMC; pursuing plug-in hybrids (DM-i) alongside BEVs when rivals treated hybrids as transitional; and fully exiting ICE in 2022.

Wang is still closely involved in engineering decisions — BYD's own communications describe him as directly engaged in R&D, and he personally presented the 2026 Blade Battery 2.0 launch [19][35]. Munger famously compared him to a combination of Edison, Ford and Gates [31]. Wang chairs BYD Electronic and sits on the boards of Denza and the BYD Charity Foundation [18]. His 2025 remuneration was RMB 8.14m, notably below several of his lieutenants [21]. He is BYD's largest individual shareholder and a member of the Chinese Communist Party [16].

**Assessment:** Wang's track record of long-horizon technology bets is exceptional, but the company's dependence on him is high, and there is no publicly identified successor. The 2025 sales-target miss (4.6m vs. 5.5m) and the overbuilding of domestic capacity going into the 2026 downturn are the clearest recent misjudgements.

### Zhou Yalin — Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer

Zhou Yalin, 49, is a career BYD finance executive who has spent her entire professional life at the company. Born in 1977, she graduated from Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics and joined what was then BYD Industrial in 1999 in a junior accounting role [20a]. She was promoted to Chief Accountant in November 2014, appointed CFO in 2019 and elevated to Senior Vice President in 2021, giving her oversight of group financial strategy [21].

Her remit extends well beyond the parent's accounts. Zhou is also CFO of BYD Electronic, a director of BYD Semiconductor, and chairman of both BYD Auto Finance and BYD Property & Casualty Insurance [21]. She has been closely involved in BYD's capital-markets activity — including its 2025 Hong Kong share placement — as well as its industrial finance and overseas expansion funding.

Her 2025 pre-tax remuneration of RMB 10.14m made her the first CFO of an A-share listed company to exceed RMB 10m, up from RMB 8.96m in 2024 and roughly eightfold over a decade [21][20a].

**Assessment:** Zhou is credible and deeply institutional, but the balance-sheet trends under her watch now deserve scrutiny: BYD shifted from net cash to net debt in 2025 [7], short-term borrowings rose sharply in Q1 2026 [12a], operating cash flow roughly halved in 2025 [8], and inventories reached RMB 221.6bn at end-September 2026 (+60% since December) [2]. Investors will want clearer disclosure on supplier payables, working capital and overseas inventory in transit.

### Stella Li (Li Ke) — Executive Vice President; CEO of BYD Americas, Europe, Middle East and Africa

Stella Li, 57, is the executive most responsible for BYD's internationalisation — the single most important driver of the investment case today. Born in 1969 in Hubei, she graduated in statistics from Fudan University in 1992 and joined BYD in 1996 as marketing manager [22][23]. She led negotiations with early customers such as Samsung, Motorola and Nokia, and was in charge of marketing and sales for cellular components and modules from 2002 to 2006 [22].

Li has built nearly all of BYD's overseas footprint. She founded its first overseas office in Hong Kong in 1997, its European headquarters in Rotterdam in 1999 and its first North American office in Chicago [22][23]. Appointed President of BYD Americas in February 2010, she moved its headquarters to Los Angeles in 2011 and established electric bus, truck and battery factories in Lancaster, California, in 2013, before expanding BYD into more than 20 countries across the Americas [22]. She previously served as Executive Director and CEO of BYD Electronic. In May 2024 she took over responsibility for BYD's European business [23], which has since become its fastest-growing region; she is now the public face of the Hungarian plant ramp and the search for a second European site [25].

Li was named 2025 World Car Person of the Year — the first woman to receive the award — and was reportedly instrumental in persuading Berkshire Hathaway to invest [22][23]. Her 2025 remuneration of RMB 14.26m was the highest among BYD executives [20a].

**Assessment:** Li has a strong record of opening markets, but Europe has also exposed execution gaps — Reuters reported in 2025 that BYD's early European rollout suffered from insufficient dealer sign-ups and a lack of locally experienced managers [26] — and the Hungarian plant is running roughly a year late.

### Lian Yubo — Executive Vice President, Chief Scientist, Chief Automotive Engineer and President of the Automotive Engineering Research Institute

Lian Yubo is the technical architect of BYD's vehicles and is often described within the industry as the engineer behind Wang Chuanfu [24]. He holds a master's degree from Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics [24a]. Before BYD he worked at SAIC's Yizheng Automobile, where he became deputy chief engineer at the age of 27 after training at the China Automotive Technology and Research Center and in Italy, and later served as deputy general manager at Shanghai Tongji Tongjie Automobile Design [24][24b].

Lian joined BYD at the end of 2003 to lead new-vehicle development when the company's auto R&D team was small and under-equipped [24]. He led the development of the F3, F3R and F6, which established BYD in the Chinese market, and has since overseen the company's NEV platforms (including the e-Platform 3.0 and DM-i/DM-p hybrid systems), its electric drive and thermal-management technologies, and its ADAS roadmap. He holds numerous patents in power-drive, braking and thermal-management systems [24c]. He also sits on the boards of Denza and BYD Toyota EV Technology, BYD's EV joint venture with Toyota [24b].

In November 2025 Lian was elected an academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering (Mechanical and Vehicle Engineering Division) — one of the highest honours for an engineer in China — alongside CATL's chief scientist Wu Kai [24d]. In April 2026 he argued publicly that industry competition must shift from scale expansion to value creation [24e], a theme consistent with BYD's 2026 emphasis on premium brands and flash charging.

### Other key executives

- **Lu Xiangyang** — co-founder and Vice Chairman; Wang's cousin and an early financier [27].
- **He Zhiqi** — Executive Vice President; COO of the passenger-car division; responsible for manufacturing and the domestic operating machine [27].
- **He Long** — Executive Vice President; Chairman of FinDreams Battery, BYD's battery subsidiary [27].

### Governance and ownership

BYD is a joint-stock company with H-shares (Hong Kong) and A-shares (Shenzhen). The board is controlled by executive directors with a minority of independent non-executives, consistent with Chinese practice. Wang Chuanfu and Lu Xiangyang are the largest individual shareholders. Berkshire Hathaway, historically the fourth-largest shareholder with close to 20% of H-shares, fully exited during 2022–2025 [30][31]. Following a 2025 capitalisation issue, BYD has roughly 9.1 billion shares outstanding [36].

Executive pay is high by A-share standards — several executives earned more than RMB 10m in 2025 [20a] — but modest relative to global auto peers. The key governance risks are founder concentration, related-party dealings with listed subsidiaries (BYD Electronic, BYD Semiconductor) and limited segment disclosure: BYD does not separately disclose auto profitability by region, which makes the overseas-margin thesis hard to verify precisely.

## 4. PRODUCTS & SERVICES

### Passenger vehicle brands

BYD runs a five-brand architecture spanning roughly RMB 55,000 to over RMB 1m:

| Brand | Positioning | Representative models | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BYD – Dynasty series** | Mass market, "Chinese heritage" design | Qin, Han, Tang, Song, Yuan | Largest volume series; dealer network 1 |
| **BYD – Ocean series** | Mass market, younger buyers | Seagull (Dolphin Mini/Surf), Dolphin, Seal, Sealion, Atto 2/3 | Core export range |
| **Denza** | Premium (RMB 250k–500k) | Z9GT, N9, N8L, D9 MPV | Formerly Daimler JV; now wholly owned |
| **Fangchengbao** | Personalised / off-road | Bao 5, Bao 8, Titanium 3, Formula S | Passed 600,000 cumulative sales in 2026 [15] |
| **Yangwang** | Ultra-luxury (RMB 1m+) | U8, U9, U7 | Technology halo; tiny volumes |

Together, Denza, Fangchengbao and Yangwang sold about 397,000 units in 2025, up 109%, nearly doubling their share of BYD's mix [6]. Raising premium mix is management's main lever to offset mass-market price erosion.

BYD also sells a **pickup** (Shark 6, export-only) and runs commercial vehicle lines — electric buses, coaches, trucks, forklifts and SkyRail/SkyShuttle transit systems. Commercial NEV sales were 7,043 units in October 2026 (+45%) [14a].

### Powertrain mix

BYD's dual BEV/PHEV strategy is a structural differentiator. In October 2026 it sold 305,743 BEV passenger cars (+37%, a record) and 177,382 PHEVs [14][14b]. Year to September, BEV passenger and pickup sales were 1.63 million (+1.5%) while PHEVs were 1.45 million (−10%) [13]. The mix is shifting back toward BEVs domestically as PHEV demand weakens in China, while PHEVs are increasingly important in Europe (where they avoid the BEV anti-subsidy duty) and emerging markets with thin charging infrastructure.

### Core technologies

- **Blade Battery 2.0 and FLASH charging (March 2026):** BYD claims 10–70% charge in 5 minutes and 10–97% in 9 minutes, ~5% higher energy density than the first generation, and 30–50% faster charging even on standard chargers [19][20]. BYD planned 20,000 flash-charging stations in China by end-2026 and offers a year of free charging on equipped vehicles [20][20b]. The launch has also created supply constraints: BYD's H1 2026 sales were hit by shortages during the switchover to second-generation cells [9a].
- **DM-i / DM-p hybrids:** fifth-generation DM-i offers very low fuel consumption and long combined range, which underpins BYD's PHEV cost leadership.
- **God's Eye ADAS:** a three-tier driver-assistance suite (A with lidar for premium models, B mid-tier, C camera/radar for mass market) made standard across almost the entire range in 2025 [28]. In 2026 BYD added accident compensation coverage for its urban navigation feature and offered upgrades to tier B for about RMB 12,000 [28a].
- **e-Platform 3.0 / Super e-Platform:** 800V-class architecture with integrated "8-in-1" electric drive.
- **In-house semiconductors:** BYD Semiconductor supplies IGBTs and SiC modules.

### Batteries and energy storage

FinDreams Battery supplies BYD's own vehicles and a growing roster of external customers. BYD held 15.2% of global EV battery installations in January–September 2026, second only to CATL at 39.1% [15]. Its share has eroded from about 17% in H1 2025 to 14.4% in H1 2026 because its own vehicle volumes fell while CATL grew with the wider market [33].

In stationary storage, BYD shipped more than 60 GWh of systems in 2025, overtaking Tesla (46.7 GWh) to become the world's largest BESS integrator with roughly 13% share [34]. Its HaoHan system (14.5 MWh per standard unit) and a 12.5 GWh contract with Saudi Electricity Company — among the largest grid-scale storage projects globally — anchor the business [34].

### Electronics (BYD Electronic)

BYD Electronic manufactures smartphone and tablet components and assembly (including for Apple and Android OEMs), and is expanding into AI-server components and automotive electronics. It is lower-margin (gross margin ~6% in H2 2025) and has been pressured by product-mix shifts and FX [9b].

### Pricing and differentiation

BYD's pricing strategy is to offer more technology per yuan than competitors — standard ADAS, faster charging and larger batteries at the same or lower prices — and to use its cost position to reprice the market when needed. That strategy has won share but has also been the main driver of the Chinese price war. Average discounts across the industry reached a record ~10% in March 2026 [12a]. Overseas, BYD typically prices well above its Chinese levels but below comparable local models, which is how it can absorb a 27% EU tariff on China-built BEVs and still gain share [26d].

## 5. CUSTOMERS & GO-TO-MARKET

### Customer segments

- **Chinese retail consumers** (~58% of 10M 2026 units, down from ~77% in 2025): historically concentrated in the RMB 100,000–200,000 mass market and increasingly in lower-tier cities. This segment is the most price-sensitive and most affected by the 2026 purchase-tax change.
- **Overseas retail consumers** (~42% of 10M 2026 units): Europe, Latin America (Brazil, Mexico, Chile), Southeast Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia), Australia/New Zealand, the Middle East, Japan, South Korea, Turkey and Central Asia.
- **Fleet and commercial customers:** taxi and ride-hailing fleets, municipal transit operators (electric buses), logistics fleets.
- **B2B battery and storage customers:** other automakers (BYD supplies cells to several OEMs), utilities and project developers (BESS).
- **Electronics OEMs** (via BYD Electronic).

There is no meaningful customer concentration in the auto business; the energy-storage and electronics businesses are more concentrated.

### Distribution

- **China:** franchised dealer networks organised by series and brand (Dynasty, Ocean, Denza, Fangchengbao, Yangwang). Dealer financial health is a live issue: Goldman Sachs attributed part of the Q3 2026 revenue shortfall to heavier dealer discounts on older-generation models [3].
- **Overseas:** a mix of importer/distributor partnerships and wholly owned national sales companies. In Germany BYD bought distributor Hedin Electric Mobility in 2024 to take direct control [26b]. By October 2026 BYD was present in 38 European markets [26c].

### Logistics as a competitive weapon

BYD operates its own fleet of roll-on/roll-off car carriers — eight vessels were in service by early 2026 [6]. Management has said shipping capacity was a binding constraint on 2026 overseas volumes, and a larger fleet is central to its 2027 target [25a].

### Overseas targets and progress

BYD's official 2026 overseas target was 1.3 million units [25b], but the company is on track for close to 2.0 million and has set a 2.5 million target for 2027 [25a][13]. Per-vehicle profitability overseas is materially higher than in China: analysts estimate domestic profit at under RMB 3,000 per car versus a multiple of that abroad [25a].

### Key partnerships

- **Toyota:** BYD Toyota EV Technology JV for co-developed EVs in China.
- **Saudi Electricity Company:** 12.5 GWh BESS project [34].
- **Stellantis and other European OEMs:** BYD has held talks about taking over idle European factories for a second plant [25].

### Case study: Europe

Europe illustrates both the opportunity and the friction. BYD's EU registrations rose 154% to 206,002 in the first nine months of 2026, lifting its EU share to 2.4% from 1.0% a year earlier [26c]. In H1 2026 BYD matched Tesla's share across the EU, EFTA and UK at 2.4% [26d]. It achieved this despite a 27% total tariff on its China-built BEVs (17% countervailing duty plus 10% base) by leaning on PHEVs, which carry only the 10% base duty: around 70% of BYD's German registrations are PHEVs, and BYD became Germany's best-selling PHEV brand in May 2026 [32a]. That workaround is now under threat (see Risk Assessment).

## 6. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

### Definition and scope

BYD competes primarily in the global light-vehicle market, specifically in the "new energy vehicle" segment (BEV + PHEV, plus extended-range EVs in China). Adjacent markets are EV batteries and stationary energy storage.

### Global EV market

Global EV sales (BEV + PHEV) reached about 15.5 million units in January–September 2026, up only 3% year on year after 26% growth in the same period of 2025 [37][37a]. Regional divergence is extreme:

- **China:** still the largest market (1.11 million EVs in September 2026), but down 13% year to date, with PHEVs down 25% and BEVs down 6% [37].
- **Europe:** record sales of about 3.9 million year to date (+30%) [37a].
- **North America:** sharply lower after the US federal EV tax credit expired at the end of September 2025; North America was down 44% in September 2026 [37].
- **Rest of world:** the fastest-growing region — 2.3 million year to date, up 93% [37a] — and BYD's sweet spot.

### China: from growth engine to shrinking market

China's NEV sales grew 28% to 16.49 million units in 2025 [7], but 2026 has been the hardest year for the domestic industry since the NEV boom began:

- **Policy cliff:** the full NEV purchase-tax exemption expired at end-2025, replaced by a 5% levy (half the standard 10%) with relief capped at RMB 15,000 per car [38a]. The trade-in subsidy programme was extended but restructured into percentage-based subsidies [38].
- **Demand pull-forward:** buyers accelerated purchases into Q4 2025, deepening the Q1 2026 slump [12].
- **Result:** domestic light passenger retail sales fell about 20% to 8.70 million in H1 2026 [38b]; GlobalData now expects a 12% decline for full-year 2026 to 23.5 million [38c].
- **Exports:** China's vehicle exports rose 76% to 7.0 million units year to date, with NEV exports up 139% to 3.9 million [32c]. Exports are the industry's release valve — and the source of rising trade friction.

The domestic market did stabilise in the autumn: CPCA estimated October 2026 NEV wholesale at a record 1.8 million units [14c].

### Industry structure and dynamics

- **Overcapacity and "involution":** China has far more NEV capacity than domestic demand. Beijing has framed the price war as "involution" and is now using policy (pricing guidance, payment-term rules for suppliers, export-rebate cuts) to force consolidation [32b].
- **Consolidation:** weaker brands are exiting; Huawei's HIMA alliance deliveries fell 51% year on year in October 2026 [14d], while scale players and well-funded challengers (Geely, Chery, Leapmotor, Xiaomi) gained.
- **Technology commoditisation:** features once reserved for premium vehicles — ADAS, fast charging, large screens — are now standard across price points, compressing differentiation and making cost the decisive variable.
- **Barriers to entry:** high (capital intensity, battery supply, software, distribution, regulatory homologation), but Chinese tech companies (Xiaomi, Huawei) have shown that well-capitalised entrants can scale quickly.

### Battery and energy-storage dynamics

The battery supply chain is even more concentrated than vehicles. Global EV battery installations reached 844 GWh in January–August 2026 (+20%), seven of the top ten suppliers are Chinese with 73% combined share, and LFP has risen to 57% of the market as automakers favour cost and safety over peak energy density [33a]. This plays to BYD's chemistry choice, but CATL — supplying almost every automaker — has captured most of the incremental growth.

Stationary storage is the fastest-growing adjacent market: global BESS installations rose about 51% to roughly 315 GWh in 2025, with cell shipments nearly doubling to more than 600 GWh and eight of the top ten system integrators Chinese [34]. Overcapacity is building here too — BESS cell capacity is expected to exceed 1.7 TWh by end-2026 against shipments of around 800 GWh — so pricing pressure is likely to follow the EV pattern [34b].

### Key structural trends to 2030

1. **Shift of growth from China to the rest of the world.** China's domestic NEV market is maturing at >50% penetration, while emerging markets are growing at close to 100% a year from a low base [37a].
2. **Localisation behind tariff walls.** Trade policy is pushing Chinese OEMs to build plants in Europe, Turkey, Brazil, Southeast Asia and Central Asia.
3. **Charging speed as the new battleground.** Megawatt-class charging (BYD's FLASH, rivals' equivalents) is replacing range as the key differentiator.
4. **ADAS democratisation.** Assisted driving is becoming standard on mass-market cars, shifting competition toward data scale and software.
5. **Policy-led consolidation in China.** Rebate cuts and anti-"involution" guidance should reduce the number of sub-scale players over time.

### Regulatory environment

- **China:** purchase-tax policy (5% in 2026–27), trade-in subsidies, new battery safety standard GB 38031-2025 (which Blade 2.0 exceeds [19a]), and the NEV export VAT rebate cut from 13% to 9% from 1 April 2027 [32]. Battery export rebates fell to 6% in April 2026 and will be eliminated from January 2027 [32].
- **EU:** countervailing duties on China-built BEVs since October 2024 (17% for BYD) [32a]; a framework for minimum-price undertakings agreed in January 2026 [32d]; an anti-subsidy process extending duties to Chinese PHEVs advanced in mid-2026 [32e]; and Brussels pressing Beijing to cap hybrid exports [32f].
- **US:** effectively closed to Chinese EVs through tariffs and connected-vehicle rules.
- **Emerging markets:** a mix of tariff walls with local-production incentives (Brazil, Turkey, Indonesia, India), which is driving BYD's overseas plant programme.

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

BYD remains the clear volume leader but its relative position has weakened in China during 2026 while strengthening abroad. October 2026 China passenger NEV wholesale rankings were: BYD 483,125; Geely 213,165; Chery 134,471; Changan 122,422; Leapmotor 103,021 [14c].

### Competitor analysis

**1. Geely Automobile (0175.HK) — the strongest domestic challenger.** Geely's NEV sales rose 21% to 214,996 in October 2026, a record 69% of its total [14a]. It targets 3.45 million total sales and 2.22 million NEVs in 2026 [39]. Geely's Galaxy sub-brand is BYD's most direct competitor in the mass market, and Geely is pairing it with Zeekr and Lynk & Co at the premium end. Geely is also expanding abroad and faces a similar EU BEV duty (28.8% total) [32g]. *Threat level: high, domestically.*

**2. Chery Automobile (9973.HK) — the export specialist.** Chery sold a record 140,893 NEVs in October and exported more than 200,000 vehicles of all types in the month [14a][14d]. It targets 3.2 million total sales in 2026 [39]. Chery's export network (Russia, Middle East, Latin America, Europe via Omoda/Jaecoo) overlaps heavily with BYD's growth markets, and its Jaecoo 7 PHEV competes directly with BYD in the UK and EU [32h]. *Threat level: high, overseas.*

**3. Tesla (TSLA) — global BEV peer.** Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3 2026 (−2% y/y) and 1.32 million year to date (+9%) [40]. BYD outsold Tesla by roughly 276,000 EVs in Q3 alone [40a]. Tesla retains advantages in software, autonomy ambitions and its Supercharger network, but lacks a sub-US$30,000 model and has lost share in both China and Europe. *Threat level: moderate.*

**4. Leapmotor (9863.HK) — value-focused challenger backed by Stellantis.** Leapmotor delivered 597,000 vehicles in 2025 (+103%) and targets 1 million in 2026 [39a]; it set a monthly delivery record of more than 106,000 in October [14e]. The Stellantis partnership gives it access to European distribution and potentially European factories. *Threat level: moderate-high in the RMB 100–200k segment.*

**5. SAIC Motor (incl. MG and SAIC-GM-Wuling).** SAIC's NEV sales were around 259,000 in October (+25%), placing it second to BYD at group level, with exports of 163,000 vehicles of all types [14d]. MG is a strong export brand in Europe and Asia but faces the highest EU BEV duty (35.3%) [32g]. *Threat level: moderate.*

**6. Changan Automobile (Deepal, Avatr, Qiyuan).** Changan targets 3.3 million sales and 1.4 million NEVs in 2026 plus 750,000 exports [39b]. Its Qiyuan brand moved into the top five NEV brands in October [14d]. *Threat level: moderate.*

**7. Xiaomi Auto.** Xiaomi delivered 410,000 vehicles in 2025 and targets 550,000 in 2026 [39a]. It exceeded 50,000 in October 2026 for the first time this year [14d]. Xiaomi competes on brand, software ecosystem and design at the RMB 200k+ level — the segment BYD is targeting with Denza and its higher-trim BYD models. *Threat level: moderate, in premium.*

**8. CATL (3750.HK / 300750.SZ) — battery competitor.** CATL held 39.1% of global EV battery installations in January–September 2026 versus BYD's 15.2% [15], and is the default supplier to most non-BYD automakers globally. It also competes in stationary storage. *Threat level: high in batteries; limits BYD's external battery growth.*

**9. Legacy global OEMs (Volkswagen, Toyota, Hyundai-Kia, Stellantis).** These compete with BYD in export markets. Volkswagen leads the EU market and reports electric models at about 40% of its European order book [37]. Toyota competes in hybrids in Southeast Asia and emerging markets. *Threat level: moderate overseas; low in China.*

## 8. MARKET OPPORTUNITY (TAM)

### Methodology

We size BYD's opportunity bottom-up across three addressable markets: (1) global passenger NEVs, (2) EV batteries sold externally, (3) stationary energy storage. Figures are rounded, use the most recent third-party data available, and are intended to frame (not replace) Task 2 assumptions.

### 1. Passenger vehicles — the core TAM

- **Total global light-vehicle market:** roughly 90 million units a year. China alone is about 23.5–25.7 million units in 2026 depending on forecaster [38c][38d].
- **Global EV market (TAM):** about 20–21 million units in 2026 on current run-rates (15.5 million in 9M 2026 [37a]). At an average BYD-like transaction price of ~US$20,000–25,000, that is a ~US$450–500bn annual revenue pool.
- **EV penetration headroom:** China NEV penetration is above 50% [24e]; Europe is around one-third; most emerging markets are still in single digits. The long-run TAM is therefore the full ~90 million-unit market as penetration rises.

### 2. Serviceable addressable market (SAM)

Excluding markets effectively closed to BYD (the US; partly India), BYD's SAM is roughly:

| Region | Annual light-vehicle market (approx.) | EV penetration trend | BYD access |
|---|---|---|---|
| China | 23–26m | >50% NEV | Full |
| Europe (EU+EFTA+UK) | ~13–14m | ~⅓ NEV | Tariffed; local plant from Q4 2026 |
| Latin America | ~5–6m | Low but rising fast | Brazil plant; strong share |
| Southeast Asia & Oceania | ~5m | Low–mid | Thai, Indonesian plants |
| Middle East, Africa, Central Asia | ~6m | Low | Exports; Uzbekistan plant |
| Japan & Korea | ~6–7m | Low | Small, brand-building |

Taken together, BYD's SAM is around 60–65 million vehicles a year, of which perhaps 25–30 million are electrified today.

### 3. Serviceable obtainable market (SOM) and share

- **China:** BYD's domestic NEV share has fallen with its domestic volumes in 2026. Holding roughly 2.5–3.0 million domestic units against an NEV market of ~12–14 million implies a sustainable share of around 20–25%.
- **Overseas:** BYD is on track for ~2.0 million units in 2026 and targets 2.5 million in 2027 [25a]. On a 35–40 million-unit addressable overseas market (ex-US), 2.5 million would be about 6–7% share — plausible given BYD's existing share in markets such as Brazil, Thailand and Australia, but dependent on tariffs.
- **Combined:** a 5.0–5.5 million-unit run-rate by 2027–28 is achievable, consistent with market chatter around BYD's original 2026 internal target of 5–5.5 million [39c].

### 4. Batteries and storage (adjacent TAM)

- **EV batteries:** global installations reached 844 GWh in January–August 2026 (+20%) [33a], putting 2026 on course for ~1.3 TWh. BYD's external battery sales are a small slice; most of its 15% share is captive.
- **Energy storage:** global BESS installations grew 51% to about 315 GWh in 2025 [34], with cell shipments over 600 GWh; industry participants expect 30–50% growth in 2026 [34a]. At BYD's ~13% integrator share, the storage business is a credible multi-billion-dollar profit pool with less tariff exposure than vehicles.

### Penetration strategy

BYD's path to its SOM rests on: (a) localised production behind tariff walls (Brazil, Hungary, Thailand, Indonesia, Turkey); (b) more premium and PHEV products in Europe; (c) flash charging as a differentiated ecosystem in China; and (d) export volume using its own shipping fleet.

## 9. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Company-specific risks

**1. Domestic price war and margin erosion (High likelihood / High severity).** BYD's domestic economics are thin: H1 2026 domestic gross margin was 15.7%, down 1.3 points year on year [10], and analyst estimates put domestic per-vehicle profit well below RMB 3,000 [25a]. Further price cuts from Geely, Leapmotor or Xiaomi — or BYD's own model-year changeovers, which drove heavier dealer discounts in Q3 [3] — could push domestic auto close to breakeven. *Mitigants:* premium-brand mix, Blade 2.0 differentiation, regulatory pressure against "involution."

**2. Working capital and inventory build (Medium / High).** Inventories rose 60% to RMB 221.6bn between December 2025 and September 2026, which BYD attributes mainly to overseas growth (vehicles in transit and in overseas dealer stock) [2]. Operating cash flow roughly halved in 2025 and the balance sheet moved from net cash to net debt [7][8]. If overseas sell-through slows, BYD may need to discount abroad or cut production. *Mitigant:* large undrawn bank facilities and access to equity markets.

**3. Overseas execution and plant delays (Medium / Medium).** The Hungarian plant is about a year late, the Turkish plant is on hold, and the Brazilian site has faced litigation over alleged labour abuses involving contractors [25][25c]. Earlier European missteps included thin dealer coverage and limited local management [26]. Delays defer tariff-free capacity precisely when EU duties are expanding.

**4. Key-person dependency (Low / High).** Wang Chuanfu, 60, has run BYD for 31 years with no named successor. The senior bench (Zhou, Li, Lian, He Zhiqi, He Long) is deep and long-tenured, but strategic decision-making is highly centralised.

**5. Technology ramp risk (Medium / Medium).** The Blade 2.0 switchover caused supply shortages in H1 2026 [9a], and the 20,000-station flash-charging network requires heavy capital outlay with uncertain monetisation (a year of free charging is bundled with equipped cars) [20b].

### Industry / market risks

**6. EU tariffs on PHEVs and minimum pricing (High / High).** The EU's anti-subsidy work on Chinese PHEVs advanced in 2026 [32e]; if BYD's PHEVs attract a duty similar to its 17% BEV rate, its main tariff workaround in Europe disappears. Chinese brands held 28.3% of Europe's PHEV market in H1 2026 [32f]. *Mitigants:* the Szeged plant (BEV and PHEV) and the minimum-price undertaking route.

**7. China policy dependence (High / Medium).** The 2026 purchase-tax change cut domestic demand sharply; the scheduled return to the full 10% purchase tax (expected after 2027) and the scale of future trade-in funding are additional cliffs.

**8. Competitive intensity from well-funded challengers (High / Medium).** Geely, Chery, Leapmotor, Xiaomi and Changan all set double-digit growth targets for 2026 [39], while Huawei-linked brands and Tesla compete at the top. Share loss in China has already been significant in 2026.

**9. Technology disruption (Low–Medium / Medium).** Solid-state or sodium-ion batteries, or a step-change in autonomy from rivals, could erode BYD's battery-cost and value-for-money advantages.

### Financial risks

**10. Export VAT rebate cut and loss of overseas margin (High / Medium).** China will cut the NEV export VAT rebate from 13% to 9% from 1 April 2027, and battery export rebates disappear from January 2027 [32]. With roughly 40–50% of revenue now overseas, this directly lowers the export margin that has driven 2026's profit recovery. *Quantification:* a 4-point rebate cut on overseas auto revenue of roughly RMB 350–400bn implies an annual headwind on the order of RMB 10bn+ before pricing actions — a key Task 2 sensitivity.

**11. FX exposure (Medium / Medium).** FX losses were a major cause of the H1 2026 profit decline [9]; 9M 2026 finance expenses were RMB 7.37bn versus net finance income of RMB 2.94bn a year earlier [1]. A stronger renminbi against the euro, real and baht hurts translated export earnings.

**12. Capex and leverage (Medium / Medium).** Simultaneous investment in overseas plants, shipping, charging and R&D (RMB 63bn in 2025) while profits fall raises leverage. Short-term borrowings rose sharply in Q1 2026 [12a].

### Macroeconomic risks

**13. Chinese consumer weakness (Medium / High).** Property-market stress and weak consumer confidence underpin the domestic slump; GlobalData forecasts a further decline in 2027 [38d].

**14. Geopolitics and trade fragmentation (Medium / High).** US exclusion is effectively permanent; EU, Turkish, Brazilian, Mexican and other markets can tighten rules quickly. BYD's export-led model is exposed to a broader backlash against Chinese overcapacity.

**15. Commodity prices (Low–Medium / Medium).** LFP reduces exposure to nickel and cobalt, but lithium carbonate price spikes would raise cell costs.
