**We initiate on BYD with a HOLD rating and a HK$78 price target.** The target is a weighted blend of a discounted cash flow valuation (60% weight, HK$94.7), an EV/EBITDA peer valuation (25%, HK$71.4) and a P/E peer valuation (15%, HK$22.6). The weighted value of HK$78.1 is rounded to HK$78.

The two approaches tell different stories, and that tension is the investment case:

- **On intrinsic value BYD looks cheap.** Our DCF of HK$94.7 implies 31% upside. It credits BYD for doubling overseas volumes to 3.25 million units by 2030, a mix shift toward higher-margin export markets, and free cash flow of RMB 45–50bn a year from 2027 once the 2026 inventory build unwinds.
- **On relative value BYD looks expensive.** At 18.0x our 2027E EPS and 17.2x trailing earnings, BYD trades at roughly twice the P/E of Chinese peers (Geely, Chery and Great Wall at 5.5–5.7x forward). Those peers have large net cash balances and similar or faster near-term earnings growth. On EV/EBITDA, BYD (4.8x trailing) sits only 3% above the peer median, because its heavy depreciation depresses its P/E relative to cash generation.

**Why HOLD, not BUY.** Our 2027E net profit of RMB 31.4bn is about 20% below consensus. Consensus forward P/E of ~14.1x at HK$70.55 implies forward EPS of about RMB 4.27, against our RMB 3.43. The gap comes from three things we explicitly model and the market may be underpricing:

1. China's NEV export VAT rebate falls from 13% to 9% from April 2027. Net of 50% pass-through, this is a RMB 5–7bn annual cost.
2. Domestic volumes keep declining (China sales −28% year to date).
3. The working-capital cost of the overseas pipeline (inventory +60% since December).

Until we see evidence that overseas profit per car survives the rebate cut and EU duties on plug-in hybrids, we see the risk/reward as balanced.

**What would make us more positive:** visible margin resilience in 1H-2027 exports, the Hungarian plant ramping on schedule, or a domestic price-war truce under Beijing's "anti-involution" agenda. These are the drivers of our Bull case (HK$132). **What would make us negative:** EU PHEV duties at or above the 17% BEV rate, or a second leg of domestic price cuts. These are the drivers of our Bear case (HK$57).

**Why these peers:**
- **Geely, Chery, Great Wall and Leapmotor** are BYD's direct mass-market competitors in China and increasingly abroad. They are the primary set.
- **Li Auto and Tesla** are EV pure-play references. Both screen as NM, Li Auto because it is loss-making and Tesla because autonomy option value inflates its multiples.
- **Toyota and Volkswagen** are the global scale incumbents BYD competes with in Europe, Southeast Asia and Latin America. Their EVs include captive-finance debt, which overstates EV/EBITDA.

### 3.2 Positioning versus peers

- **EV/EBITDA:** BYD trades at 4.8x trailing, essentially at the peer median (+3%). Its EBITDA margin of 14.2% is more than double the Chinese peers' 5–6%, because D&A is about 10% of revenue at BYD's integration and capex intensity. On this cash-earnings measure BYD is not expensive.
- **P/E:** BYD trades at 17–18x, a 120% premium to the median. That premium was historically justified by superior growth. But consensus now has Geely and Chery growing revenue at 13–15% a year, against our 5.8% for BYD, and both hold large net cash. BYD's earnings premium therefore rests on overseas margin durability, which is exactly what the 2027 policy changes challenge.

### 3.4 Precedent transactions

Not applied. There have been no control transactions at comparable scale in Chinese autos: the major OEMs are founder- or state-controlled, and recent deals (for example Stellantis–Leapmotor) were minority strategic stakes, not takeovers. No takeover premium is assumed.

## 5. INVESTMENT RECOMMENDATION

**HOLD, price target HK$78 (12 months), total return +9.4%.**

BYD remains the world's most cost-advantaged EV maker, with leading positions in NEVs (4.6 million units in 2025) and stationary storage (No. 1 BESS integrator in 2025). Its export pivot is real:
- overseas revenue exceeded domestic revenue for the first time in 1H-26;
- exports were up 103% year on year in October;
- overseas gross margin was 21.7% against 15.7% domestically.

But three things argue against chasing the stock until there is more evidence:
1. **Policy reprices the export profit pool.** The April 2027 VAT rebate cut and prospective EU PHEV duties both hit the export business that is driving the recovery.
2. **The domestic franchise is still shrinking.** China sales are down 28% year to date, with share loss to Geely, Leapmotor and Xiaomi.
3. **Earnings quality has weakened.** The Q3 2026 beat on profit growth came with a 60% inventory build, a 17% cut in R&D and FX volatility.

Our numbers sit about 20% below consensus for 2027. If consensus converges toward us, the current 14x consensus P/E becomes 18x, leaving limited upside from here despite the stock's 24% decline this year.

## 6. KEY CATALYSTS (next 12 months)

1. **November–December 2026 monthly sales (early each month).** Q4 volume needs about 1.0 million units to reach our 2026E of 4.62 million, and export run-rate needs to hold above 170,000 a month. Upside if BYD beats its implied ~2.0 million overseas units.
2. **Hungarian (Szeged) plant start of production (Q4 2026) and second European site decision.** Local production is BYD's main defence against EU duties. Each 100,000 units of tariff-free capacity is worth roughly RMB 2–3bn of gross profit at current tariff differentials.
3. **EU decision on Chinese PHEV countervailing duties and minimum-price undertakings (expected late 2026 to 2027).** This is the largest single swing factor for European profitability, given about 70% of BYD's German registrations are PHEVs. A price-undertaking outcome would be a positive surprise.
4. **FY2026 results and 2027 targets (late March 2027).** We expect 2027 guidance of about 2.5 million overseas units. Disclosure of overseas margin after the April rebate cut and inventory normalisation are the key watch items.
5. **China policy: "anti-involution" pricing guidance and 2027 trade-in subsidy funding.** Effective curbs on discounting would support the Bull case gross margin (22–22.5%).

## 7. KEY RISKS TO THE PRICE TARGET

**Upside risks**
1. **Overseas margins prove resilient through the rebate cut** (medium probability, +15–25%). If 70% or more of the cut is passed through and the EU settles on price undertakings, 2027E EPS could move toward consensus (about RMB 4.3).
2. **Blade 2.0 / FLASH charging regains domestic share** (medium, +10–15%). BEV sales passed 300,000 units in October; a sustained domestic recovery would lift both volume and ASP.
3. **Energy storage re-rating** (low–medium, +5–10%). With BESS shipments above 60 GWh and a 12.5 GWh Saudi project, a separate disclosure or listing could highlight value not captured in auto multiples.

**Downside risks**
1. **EU PHEV duties at the BEV rate (17%+) without a price undertaking** (medium–high, –15–20%). This would push the business toward our Bear case of 2.1 million overseas units in 2027.
2. **Renewed domestic price war or slower China demand** (medium, –10–15%). Each 1pp of auto gross margin is worth about RMB 7bn of gross profit (about RMB 6bn after tax, or roughly 19% of 2027E net income).
3. **Working-capital stress** (medium, –5–10%). If inventory does not normalise to about 90 days, 2027E FCF of RMB 45bn could halve and net debt would stay above RMB 60bn.
4. **Geopolitics and FX** (low–medium, –10%+). Renminbi appreciation (FX losses hit 1H-26) and trade restrictions in Latin America or Turkey.
